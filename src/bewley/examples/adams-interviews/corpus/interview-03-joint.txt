Title: Joint interview with Abigail and John Adams (FICTIONAL)
Format: Q&A transcript with line-labeled speaker turns, two participants
Status: FICTIONAL — synthetic demonstration data written in 2026 for the
Status: bewley tutorial. This exchange never took place. Themes and some
Status: phrasing are drawn from the genuine 1775-1776 correspondence (see
Status: the adams-letters example); every answer is invented.

INTERVIEWER: I have you both together, which the war rarely managed. Let
me ask the obvious question first. Who had the harder years, 1775 and
1776?

ABIGAIL ADAMS: John will say I did, because it is gallant, and because
it is true.

JOHN ADAMS: I will say she did because the evidence says so. I risked a
hanging in the abstract; she managed smallpox in the house. She had the
children inoculated at Boston while I read about it a fortnight later,
helpless as a newspaper.

ABIGAIL ADAMS: A fortnight if the post rider was sober. I want very much
to hear from you, I wrote, more times than I can now count. Waiting for
news is its own occupation. No one gives you credit for it, and it fills
the whole day regardless.

INTERVIEWER: Did you quarrel by letter?

JOHN ADAMS: We negotiated. A quarrel wants an answer within the hour;
ours took six weeks a round, which cools the blood wonderfully.

ABIGAIL ADAMS: We quarreled once, properly, about the ladies and the new
code of laws. He laughed at me in writing. I have kept the letter, so
that history may decide which of us it finds amusing.

INTERVIEWER: And the duty itself — the Congress, the farm, the whole
machinery. Was it worth what it cost the household?

ABIGAIL ADAMS: Ask me what it cost first. It cost a decade of evenings.
The country got a constitution and I got a correspondence. I am proud of
both, but only one of them kept me company.

JOHN ADAMS: It was worth it, and it was not, and any honest founder will
tell you the same in private. The public duty was real and the private
arithmetic never balanced. We paid it in separation, and we paid it
willingly, and we would both like the record to show that willingly is
not the same word as gladly.
